FOUND THE COLONY

Click level ground to set the Umbilicus, the centre of your city. The gold stones show the pomerium, the sacred boundary. The only spring rises on the hill to the north-east; the river is nearer but fouls. Your veterans wait in their wagons.

TABULA: TILE GUIDE

Placement

Most tiles only work when a street touches them, so lay the Via first.

  • Place: pick a tile in the left menu, then click the map. Drag to lay Via, Aquaeductus and Cloaca in a line.
  • Rotate R. Demolish X (refunds 30%; a Via refunds 3). Inspect Esc, then click a building.
  • Road frontage is needed by everything except Aquaeductus, Cloaca, Caput Aquae, Castellum, Lacus, Tabernaculum and Ager.
  • Terrain: buildings cannot stand in the river. A Pons bridges it, and an Aquaeductus can stand in it. The Emporium must touch the bank. A Caput Aquae needs high ground.
  • Layers: a Cloaca runs underground beneath any tile, and anything can be built over it. An Aquaeductus can arch over a Via, and a Via can be laid under an existing arcade. Demolish removes the top layer first: building, then street or bridge, then sewer.
  • Lens cycles overlays for water, sewers, vigiles, castra, markets, temples, baths, games and patronage. Green is covered, red is not.
  • Reach is in tiles, given per tile below. The Walking range rule scales every one.
  • Colonia mode: gold dashes mark the planned grid. A Via on them costs half; other buildings over them cost 30% more.

Water and health

Water runs in one line: Caput Aquae, Aquaeductus, Castellum, then Lacus and Thermae. Each spring yields about 2,600 a month, minus leakage. The town draws about 1 per person, 400 per Thermae and 40 per Lacus.

Change of use

Blocks change as the city lives. The Lex group "Urban change" has three sliders: land-use fluidity (zero freezes everything), how readily elites lease their frontage, and how fast designated sites are cleared. A domus on a busy street may be split into a workshop and shops, or into flats if housing is short. Tenements on busy corners may become shops, and shops where trade is thin may become tenements. Owners rebuild many burnt blocks, sometimes as something else, and a rich family may build a new domus on vacant ground. Each change is logged with its reason.

Entertainment buildings are never cleared automatically. Choose Site: Theatre or Site: Arena, click the plot, and the homes on it are bought out for half their price and demolished. Displaced crowds raise unrest. When the plot is empty and you can pay, the building goes up. Click a site to cancel it for half the deposit.

Pompeii

The Pompeii map starts in January AD 62, but draws the city as it stood in AD 79: walls and seven gates, the forum and temples, two baths and a theatre, the amphitheatre in the south-east corner, water from the Aqua Augusta arriving at the castellum by the Porta Vesuvio, and lead pipes carrying it to street fountains. Several sites are still rubble from the earthquake of AD 62. One tile is about 35 metres, and a building tile stands for several houses, so the game shows about a quarter of the real population.

  • Run the city as you like. The Lex panel has a Vesuvius group: a switch for whether the mountain erupts in AD 79 (no preset touches it), the month of the eruption, and how much heed people pay to tremors. The eruption is about 148 years of play away, so use 4× speed.
  • In the three months before the eruption there are tremors. They damage buildings and cut the water pressure.
  • When the mountain erupts, the share who escape depends on the heed paid to tremors, the gates that have a road leading away, and the harbours on the Sarno. Pompeii had no vigiles, no sewers beyond a few drains and no way of knowing what the mountain was.

Build order

  1. Streets, then a few Insulae and Ager on the first blocks.
  2. Bring water: Aquaeductus from the spring, then a Castellum among the houses.
  3. Add a Cloaca from the river, and a Vigiles beside the first Insulae.
  4. Add Taberna and Officina in a cluster, then a Horreum before the first winter.
  5. Add Templum, then Forum, then Thermae as the town grows.

Common mistakes

  • A building with no adjacent Via sits idle. Inspect it to see.
  • A gap in the Aquaeductus, or a Castellum that does not touch the arcade, leaves the town dry.
  • A Cloaca that does not reach the river bank does nothing. It shows orange in the sewer view.
  • Tall Insulae without Vigiles burn in chains.
  • A lone Taberna earns less than three together in a bazaar economy.
  • Grain above the storage cap spoils. Watch the monthly grain figure in the top bar.
